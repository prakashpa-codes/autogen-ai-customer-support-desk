# Bat Care and Services

## Why English willow bats need knocking-in

A new English willow bat has soft fibres on its face. Knocking-in compresses these fibres so the bat can withstand a hard leather ball without cracking. Using a bat with a leather ball before knocking it in is the most common cause of early cracks, and that damage is not covered by the warranty.

## Knocking-in at home

1. Apply a light coat of raw linseed oil (about one teaspoon) to the face, edges and toe, avoiding the splice and handle. Let it dry flat for 24 hours.
2. Using a knocking-in mallet, tap the face gently for 6-8 hours in total, spread over several sessions, gradually increasing the force. Pay extra attention to the edges and toe, striking them at a 45-degree angle.
3. Play 30-60 minutes of gentle throw-downs or nets with an old leather ball, looking for seam marks. If deep seam marks appear, return to the mallet.
4. The bat is ready for match use when the old ball leaves only light marks.

## Oiling and storage

Oil English willow bats lightly (half a teaspoon) once at the start of the season and once mid-season. Over-oiling makes the willow heavy and dead. Store bats in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and damp; never leave a bat in a car boot in summer.
